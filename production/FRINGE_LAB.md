# Fringe lab: experimental ways to BUILD the game (no web research; pure brainstorm, 2026-10-18)
Rule for every idea: it must run headless on this container (4 CPU, no GPU, ~5 GB disk), end in a test that passes, and have a kill criterion. Development methods only. Ranked inside each tier by (originality x chance it works).

## Tier 1: cheap, weird, probably works (days)
**F1 Lint as loss: animation by optimisation.** Turn the animation lint (E9) into an objective and let CMA-ES / random-restart hill-climb *write* the key poses: parameters = ~12 bone angles for each of anticipation / impact / recovery; loss = lint violations + distance of club-tip path to a described arc + style vector (weight, snap) + anatomy joint limits + "anticipation moves the centre of mass away from the target". Deterministic, CPU-only, works on any skeleton. *First test:* regenerate Bramvex's light strike from the loss alone and compare to my hand pose in a blind side-by-side. *Kill:* optimised poses are legal but ugly in 3 of 3 tries.
**F2 Replay string = bug report.** Every bout is (seed, versions, input stream) in ~200 bytes; the build can replay it headless and render it; "bug" tickets in the repo are just replay strings. Add grammar-based and random-input FUZZING of the sim with invariants (no NaN, no stuck action, no infinite combo, no negative stamina, ring-out always resolvable). *Test:* 1M fuzzed ticks overnight, zero invariant breaks or a list of real ones.
**F3 Touch feasibility prover.** A finger model (Fitts' law, 2 thumbs, reach arcs, hold thresholds, multi-touch limits) tries to execute every move input sequence on the actual touch layout; flags combos that are physically impossible or too slow on a 6-inch phone, per left/right handedness. *Test:* it flags a deliberately bad layout and passes the current one with numbers.
**F4 Automatic highlight reels.** Fun proxies computed from sim logs (comeback swings, near-death finishes, parry chains, stall penalty) pick the best bouts of the nightly matrix; xvfb + ffmpeg renders them to 10-second clips/GIFs with the replay string in the caption. *Use:* devlog content and reviewer material produced without anyone watching hours of bouts.
**F5 Symbolic regression of balance.** Fit simple interpretable formulas from the matrix (which stats and move properties drive win rate); outputs "this lever moves win rate by about +1.3 points per 5%" so tuning is understood, not guessed.

## Tier 2: real bets (weeks)
**F6 Evolutionary move design (genetic game design).** Moves are data (startup, active, recovery, damage, range, stamina, cooldown, effects). Evolve candidate move sets per critter with the deterministic sim as the fitness function: constraints = Move Effect Gate, counterplay law, role identity; fitness = balance spread + "fun proxies" (decision density, bout-length variance, comeback rate, low stall rate) from F4's metrics. A human curates the Pareto front and picks; nothing ships unreviewed. *Success:* it finds at least one move variant per critter that beats my hand-tuned version on the fun proxies without hurting balance, and the creator prefers it blind.
**F7 Animation by acting (phone pose capture).** The creator performs an attack in front of a phone; an on-device-class pose model (MediaPipe/RTMPose family, CPU-capable) extracts body keypoints; we convert to effector paths (hand/foot trajectories + weight shift) and retarget to any critter through IK, then F1 cleans and exaggerates. Gives real human timing and weight to eight non-human bodies from a 10-second clip. *Success:* a performed overhead swing becomes a Bramvex heavy that passes lint and reads as "his". *Kill:* keypoints too noisy at phone quality even after smoothing.
**F8 Shader auto-tuning against readability.** A perceptual loss (silhouette contrast, edge density, face region contrast at quarter size) lets an optimiser tune toon-shader parameters (gain, softness, outline width, rim) per critter and per arena; CPU renders on a 12-pose sheet. *Why:* my own first Bramvex renders were over-exposed and my eye was the only judge. *Success:* automatic choice equals or beats hand-chosen values in blind review.
**F9 Lore-to-move compiler with a closed verification loop.** The lore bible and anatomy notes compile to candidate moves and poses; the sim and lint verify; failures are fed back automatically and re-generated overnight; humans only see passing candidates with a one-line "why this fits the lore". *Success:* 20 candidate abilities per critter, 5+ surviving review.
**F10 Red-team workers.** A reviewer worker with an adversarial brief (balance skeptic, animation critic, platform-policy lawyer, mobile-perf auditor) reviews each autonomous commit and can veto it by opening an issue file; cost is controlled by reviewing diffs, not whole repos. *Success:* catches a planted regression in a drill.

## Tier 3: moonshots (months; may fail, learn something either way)
**F11 Solve the abstract game.** Build a small abstraction of one-on-one combat (positions in bands, stamina/poise buckets, action phases) and solve it exactly (backward induction / fictitious play) to prove or refute laws: "no inescapable loop", "every threat has an answer within N frames", "no strictly dominant strategy". Verified against the full sim by sampling. Turns the creator's laws into theorems.
**F12 Differentiable balance.** Smooth relaxation of the sim (soft hit tests, soft action phases) in PyTorch so win rate can be differentiated with respect to frame data; gradient descent proposes tuning. Hard because combat is discrete: success is only "gradient direction agrees with the real matrix response 70% of the time".
**F13 Content-addressed deterministic asset builds.** Every asset is the pure function of (source files, script version, parameters), identified by hash; rebuilding the whole art set from the repo reproduces byte-identical GLBs; provenance falls out for free. *Why fringe:* almost no game team can rebuild its generated art bit-for-bit.
**F14 Perf prediction from a tiny model.** Predict phone frame time from (draw calls, tris, shader passes, texture MB) using the few real measurements we get from the creator's phone plus public benchmark numbers; gate CI on the prediction with an honest error bar. Needs real device data to be believable.
**F15 Worker that proposes workers.** The autonomous queue gets a planner role: once a week it reads logs, proposes new queue items and retires stale ones within a hard run budget; the creator approves the weekly plan in one message.

## What I would bet on first
F1 (lint as loss) and F2 (replays + fuzzing) because they multiply everything else and cost almost nothing; F6 (evolutionary move design) because it is the most original use of our deterministic sim; F7 (animation by acting) because it solves the hardest problem we have, human-quality weight and timing on non-human bodies. F3 and F4 are cheap wins that protect the creator's limited time.

## Guardrails for fringe work
No experiment changes shipped gameplay data without the creator's OK; every spike lives in `v2/tools/lab/` until it passes its test; every result (including failures) gets two lines in `production/RND_LOG.md`.
