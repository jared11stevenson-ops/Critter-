# MASTER DIRECTIVE: EXACT 2D REFERENCE -> 3D CHARACTER RECONSTRUCTION (creator-supplied, binding; supersedes the gate numbers in FIDELITY_PROTOCOL.md for Gate 1 geometry)

You are not designing a character. You are reconstructing the supplied 2D artwork as a faithful 3D model in Blender. The reference is the absolute source of truth. Do not improve, reinterpret, make anatomically realistic, simplify to generic low-poly, or substitute what you think the creature should look like. A model that looks like the same species but does not reproduce the reference's exact SHAPE LANGUAGE is a FAILURE.

PRIORITY ORDER (never sacrifice a higher one for a lower one; never add detail to disguise wrong proportions): 1 silhouette, 2 overall proportions, 3 landmark placement, 4 primary masses, 5 shape language, 6 overlapping forms, 7 secondary forms, 8 color-region boundaries, 9 materials, 10 fine detail.

WORKFLOW: return to neutral GRAY CLAY. Materials/colors/details are ignored until geometry passes. If existing geometry is fundamentally wrong, rebuild the area (sunk cost irrelevant).

ROLES (collaborate but NO independent redesign):
1 REFERENCE ANALYST: objective landmarks in a normalized 2D coordinate system: extreme top/bottom/left/right, snout tip, snout curvature, nose bridge, eye centre and dimensions, upper-eyelid angle, brow peak, forehead transition, horn/appendage bases and tips, jaw front/rear, mouth corners, cheek boundaries, neck attachment, skull rear, major colour boundaries, major overlapping shapes; analyse NEGATIVE SPACE as carefully as positive form; classify each visible line as A form change, B overlapping geometry, C colour boundary, D stylized linework (do NOT make every boundary a separate piece of geometry). Produce a reference specification for the Modeler.
2 PRIMARY MODELER (only agent modifying geometry): follow the Analyst's measurements; start with the LARGEST CONTINUOUS MASSES, like a sculptor: CRANIUM -> MUZZLE/SNOUT -> JAW -> CHEEK -> NECK -> MAJOR APPENDAGES. Preserve the flowing shapes; reproduce exaggerated curves, overlapping graphic masses, the elongated curved snout, forehead-to-snout transition, large eye/brow relationship, black cheek/neck mass, red/orange facial structures, rear skull silhouette, thin protruding shapes, asymmetrical stylization. Strange or anatomically implausible shapes are reproduced anyway. REFERENCE FIDELITY > REALISM.
3 CAMERA/PROJECTION SPECIALIST: establish and LOCK the reference camera BEFORE detailed modeling (rotation, elevation, perspective, focal length, framing, model rotation, scale in frame). The camera is NEVER changed to make wrong geometry look right; geometry matches the camera. Keep separate diagnostic cameras.
4 VISUAL COMPARATOR: after every meaningful revision: render from the locked reference camera at the reference resolution, overlay/difference against the reference, compare silhouette, landmarks and negative spaces, and report SPECIFIC discrepancies ('snout tip ~7% too high', 'rear skull ~9% too far back', 'lower jaw too thick', 'forehead curve begins too early'), ranked by visual importance. Never 'looks close'.
5 LEAD ART DIRECTOR / GRADER: aggressively critical, scores each iteration 0-100: Silhouette 30, Proportions 20, Landmark alignment 15, Primary mass 15, Shape language 10, Overlap/negative space 10. Materials, colors and tiny details score ZERO at this stage.
GATE 1 (gray primary geometry): >= 95/100 or no progress. Below 95: name the three largest discrepancies, send to the Modeler, re-render, regrade. Do NOT lower the threshold when progress stalls: CHANGE STRATEGY.
SILHOUETTE-FIRST TEST: completely black model on a flat contrasting background vs the reference silhouette only; 'if all internal information disappeared, would these shapes still look almost identical?'
LANDMARK TEST: project the reference landmarks onto the locked-camera render, measure normalized screen-space error, correct the largest first.
DO NOT MODEL COLOR AS RANDOM GEOMETRY: each region is (1) real raised/recessed form, (2) separate anatomical structure, (3) pigmentation, (4) stylized linework: only 1 and 2 need geometry.
SHAPE LANGUAGE must survive: no generic dragon/reptile anatomy, realistic skull, random low-poly faceting, uniform polygon sizes, or symmetrical facial construction; large sweeping forms stay large and sweeping, sharp graphic changes stay sharp, thin stays thin, exaggerated stays exaggerated.
DEPTH: after the reference-camera silhouette passes, add the MINIMUM inferred depth; never let depth alter the successful reference projection; return to the locked camera often.
3D CHEATING TEST (after >= 95): rotate ~30 deg left/right, up/down: must stay coherent 3D, not a flat cardboard relief.
SECONDARY GEOMETRY GATE (only after Gate 1): eyelids, facial plates, folds, secondary horns, mouth structures, small transitions: required >= 97/100 after repeating the overlay process.
MATERIAL GATE only after geometry passes: extract the reference's major colour regions, projected boundaries match, neutral lighting at first.
FINAL VALIDATION: A silhouette (black vs reference), B clay vs reference, C colour vs reference, D rotation; the Lead must explicitly list remaining mismatches before declaring completion.
FAILURE CONDITIONS (reject the iteration): detail added to incorrect geometry; materials faking similarity; generically reptilian snout; major curves become arbitrary polygon wedges; camera moved to hide errors; anatomy prioritized over the drawing; unusual features normalized; 'close enough'; progress despite major silhouette disagreement; repeated tiny edits while a large proportional error remains. When edits stop improving similarity: STOP, diagnose (camera, topology, primary volume construction, landmark interpretation, depth assumptions, misread 2D art) and rebuild with a DIFFERENT strategy.
MOST IMPORTANT RULE: at every stage ask 'Am I reproducing what is actually visible in the reference, or what I THINK the creature should look like?' If the second: stop and correct.
BEGIN by auditing the CURRENT MODEL against the reference; do not continue its detailing pass; identify the five largest structural errors, rank by severity, correct from largest to smallest.
