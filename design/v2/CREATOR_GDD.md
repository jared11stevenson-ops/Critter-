# CRITTER — UPDATED GAMEPLAY LOOP & CORE SYSTEMS (creator's document, October 2026) — **LAW**
> **The creator declared this document LAW (2026-10-11).** Nothing in the project may contradict it. Changing it requires an explicit design revision by the creator. Every agent brief, ticket and review must check against section 14 (Core Design Laws).
(Saved verbatim in substance. Where any other doc conflicts with this one, THIS WINS. See RECONCILIATION.md for how the rest of the plan was adjusted.)

## 1. Core premise
Humanity discovers CRITTER, an enormous living world hidden at the quantum level (about eight times larger than Earth). It is inhabited by intelligent, highly diverse species living in a savage ecosystem where fighting and killing are fundamental to survival.
Humans travel into CRITTER, capture its inhabitants, and bring them back to Earth shrunken. On Earth, Critters live in specialized Habitat Jars and participate in organized gladiatorial tournaments for human entertainment.
Critters generally view Earth as an opportunity to escape the constant struggle for survival. On CRITTER they fight to survive. On Earth they fight for the opportunity to stop fighting forever.
**Death is permanent. Every individual Critter has exactly one life.**

## 2. Primary gameplay loop
1 PREPARE (manage roster, inspect habitats, allocate resources, select a Critter for an expedition) → 2 DESCEND → 3 EXPLORE (dangerous ecosystems, discover species, study behaviors, locate recruits) →
4 CONFRONT (risk an existing Critter in an encounter with a wild Critter; combat and other approaches can influence the outcome) → 5 CAPTURE (secure a new Critter and transport it back; the expedition is not complete until extraction succeeds) →
6 ESTABLISH HABITAT (the first Critter of a species becomes the founding King or Queen of its jar) → 7 ENTER TOURNAMENTS (each fight risks permanent death) → 8 ADVANCE → 9 CHAMPION TOURNAMENT →
10 CHOOSE YOUR FUTURE (permanent retirement, or continue as defending champion) → 11 RETIRE (peaceful life in species-specific habitats) → 12 DEVELOP CIVILIZATIONS (retired Critters reproduce, form families, build settlements autonomously with resources the player allocates) →
13 EXPAND (money, resources, new Critters, growing civilizations expand the interconnected Habitat Shelf) → 14 REPEAT with harder expeditions, new species, new champions, more civilizations.

## 3. Exploration and capture
- Every normal capture expedition requires the player to risk at least one Critter already in their possession; Critters can die permanently during expeditions.
- Wild Critters are intelligent individuals, not mindless collectibles. Encounters can involve fighting, observation, persuasion and other species-appropriate interactions.
- Capturing requires successfully returning to Earth. Different species inhabit different ecosystems. The world retains its established lore, geography, creatures and biological diversity.
- The initial acquisition of the player's first Critter requires a separate introductory process.
**Core rule: you must risk an existing life to acquire a new one.**

## 4. Ability-based gladiator combat
Loosely inspired by For Honor's deliberate movement, targeting, timing, counters and attack commitment. **CRITTER is NOT a weapon-based fighting game.** Each species has its own combat identity from its anatomy, biology, supernatural abilities and individual development.
Weapons are used only when they are part of a Critter's abilities.
Combat features: third-person 3D arena battles; target locking and deliberate positioning; readable attacks, counters, evasions and defensive reactions; distinct movement and animation systems for different body types; species-specific abilities and mechanics; tactical decisions based on opponent behavior; high-risk encounters where mistakes can mean permanent death; individual combat experience and career histories.
Example: Aruun is inspired by a giraffe beetle and fights using psionic mace abilities and Rage. Its combat design must preserve these three defining traits.
**Core rule: every Critter is its own fighting style.**

## 5. Tournament progression
Qualifying tournaments → advance through competitive ranks → become a Champion → qualify for the Champion Tournament → win it → choose:
- PATH A, RETIREMENT: retire permanently; return to the species-specific Habitat Jar; eligible to establish a family and reproduce; contribute to a peaceful civilization; preserve all career achievements and records.
- PATH B, TITLE DEFENSE: keep fighting; defend the title against powerful opponents; earn money, resources, reputation, prestige; keep risking permanent death; preserve title-defense history.
(Rules governing retirement after choosing additional title defenses remain to be established.)

## 6. Permanent death and individual identity
Every Critter is a persistent individual with: identity and species; physical and genetic traits; personality; combat abilities and experience; tournament record; injuries and survival history; family relationships and ancestry; retirement status; historical achievements.
If a Critter dies in combat or on an expedition it is permanently lost. No resurrection, cloning or identical replacement. The player's records retain its accomplishments. Other individuals of the species may still exist.

## 7. Habitat Jars
Each Critter species has its own dedicated Habitat Jar; species are not permanently housed together. Habitats are peaceful sanctuaries: no fighting, killing, territorial warfare or gladiatorial combat inside. Each holds a species-appropriate ecosystem and can grow into a miniature civilization: families and offspring, multiple generations, species-specific homes and settlements, resource storage, food production, social and cultural structures, autonomous construction, monuments and records.
The player does not place individual buildings or control construction. **Critters build their own habitats.**

## 8. Autonomous habitat development
The player's responsibility is to provide resources; the Critters' responsibility is to decide how to use them.
Player can: allocate money; supply construction materials; provide environmental resources; expand habitat capacity; observe population development; monitor construction and ecological conditions.
Critters independently: evaluate needs; select construction priorities; gather and organize resources; build homes and infrastructure; maintain their environment; develop settlements as populations grow. Different species produce different architectural styles and layouts; even the same species develops differently by history, resources and individuals.

## 9. Reproduction, families, generations
Retired Critters can reproduce with compatible individuals: establish families, produce offspring, raise young, develop multigenerational populations, pass inherited biological traits, establish family trees and bloodlines. Offspring are unique individuals, not copies. Inherited traits may influence appearance, physical characteristics, abilities and tendencies. Combat experience is not inherited. Breeding does not guarantee stronger offspring.
(Eligibility of Earth-born offspring for gladiatorial combat has not yet been established.)

## 10. Founding monarch system
The first Critter of each species brought to Earth automatically becomes the founding King or Queen of that species' Habitat Jar. Monarchy is by arrival order, not combat strength. The founding monarch: is the first recognized member of its species' Earth civilization; has a permanent place in the historical record; can influence the character and development of its civilization; must still earn retirement through the Champion Tournament before beginning its retired family life; can establish a royal bloodline. If it dies, its identity and status remain recorded; a later Critter cannot retroactively become the founder. (Succession after a monarch's death remains undecided.)

## 11. The interconnected Habitat Shelf
Jars are physically arranged on shelves in the player's Earthside home base (inspired by the interconnected miniature societies of The Wall from Solar Opposites). Each jar remains the home of one species. Jars can become interconnected through communication channels, resource exchange ports, observation windows and controlled passages where compatible. Interconnected habitats remain peaceful. The shelf grows into a network of miniature civilizations; the player can inspect jars, observe behavior, view family histories and watch autonomous construction. The shelf is the lasting physical record of progress.

## 12. Economy and resource allocation
Arena combat (money, resources, reputation, advancement); expeditions (new species, materials from the quantum world); habitat development (allocate money and materials to populations, who build and maintain settlements). Arena success improves living conditions of retired Critters and descendants.

## 13. Player progression
Species discovered; successful captures; tournament achievements; champions produced; titles defended; Critters retired; founding monarchs established; populations developed; generations and lineages; habitat infrastructure; shelf expansion; regions explored; individual histories. Progress is not the number of powerful creatures collected; a successful player builds a lasting collection of peaceful civilizations through survival, risk and championship victories.

## 14. Core design laws
1 CRITTER is at the quantum level, eight times larger than Earth. 2 Critters are intelligent. 3 Their homeworld is dangerous; lethal combat is normal. 4 Humans transport Critters to Earth shrunken. 5 Every species has its own Habitat Jar. 6 The first of a species brought to Earth is that jar's founding monarch. 7 Risk an existing Critter to capture another on normal expeditions. 8 Death is permanent. 9 Combat is ability-based with For Honor-inspired tactical fundamentals. 10 Tournament progression leads to championship and the Champion Tournament. 11 Winning it grants the choice to retire or defend. 12 Retirement grants permanent peaceful life and family development. 13 Retired Critters can reproduce and establish populations. 14 Critters autonomously construct their habitats. 15 Players allocate money and resources rather than placing buildings. 16 Habitats are peaceful. 17 Species-specific jars can form an interconnected shelf. 18 Identities, family histories and achievements persist. 19 The established CRITTER species, worldbuilding and biological inspirations remain the foundation. 20 New mechanics must not contradict these laws without an explicit design revision.

## 15. Short loop
PREPARE → RISK AN EXISTING CRITTER → ENTER THE QUANTUM WORLD → EXPLORE AND ENCOUNTER WILD CRITTERS → CAPTURE AND EXTRACT → ESTABLISH ITS HABITAT → ASSIGN FOUNDING MONARCH IF FIRST OF SPECIES → TOURNAMENTS → CHAMPION → CHAMPION TOURNAMENT → RETIRE OR DEFEND → RETIRED CRITTERS FORM FAMILIES → POPULATIONS GROW AND BUILD AUTONOMOUSLY → ALLOCATE MONEY AND RESOURCES → EXPAND THE HABITAT SHELF → REPEAT.
**On CRITTER, fighting means survival. On Earth, fighting means the opportunity for freedom. Every battle risks a life. Every retirement preserves one. Every surviving generation creates something that lasts beyond the arena.**
